01 · The problem and user

Make the electricity system easier for students to explore.

Renewable electricity can be difficult to understand from static charts and technical explanations alone. EduGrid is designed for secondary-school teachers and students in Ireland who want a straightforward way to explore how wind generation, solar generation and electricity demand can change in different scenarios.

Who it is for

Teachers and secondary-school students

Teachers can use the demo to introduce energy topics, while students can adjust values, compare example scenarios and discuss what they notice.

The challenge

Turning energy information into understanding

Energy figures can feel abstract when learners cannot experiment with them. EduGrid offers a simple interactive example using clearly labelled fictional data, so users can explore the relationships without mistaking the demo for a live grid service.

03 · How it works

Three simple steps from curiosity to understanding.

The concept is designed to help learners ask questions, test ideas and talk about renewable electricity.

STEP 01

Choose a scenario

Start with a preset or adjust the wind, solar and demand controls yourself.

STEP 02

Compare the result

Watch the simplified indicator change and inspect the example records beside it.

STEP 03

Discuss what you notice

Use the results to ask how weather, demand and renewable generation might interact.

Existing alternatives

Designed around learning and experimentation.

There are already useful energy information resources. EduGrid’s proposed focus is a simple, classroom-friendly scenario activity.

Alternative 1

EirGrid information and dashboards

EirGrid publishes information about Ireland’s electricity system. These resources are useful for understanding the real grid; EduGrid’s concept instead centres on fictional scenarios learners can change themselves.

Alternative 2

SEAI energy education resources

The Sustainable Energy Authority of Ireland provides educational resources about energy and sustainability. EduGrid could complement classroom materials with an interactive, adjustable scenario demo.

EduGrid is an independent student project concept and is not affiliated with or endorsed by EirGrid or SEAI.
Proposed model

A simple concept with room to grow.

The current page is a prototype. Any future version would need user testing, verified data sources and appropriate school feedback.

Who could use it?

  • Secondary-school teachers introducing energy topics
  • Students completing sustainability projects
  • School energy clubs and science activities

Possible funding approach

A future version could be offered free for basic classroom use, with development supported by education or sustainability grants, school partnerships, or sponsored learning resources. These are ideas, not current funding arrangements.
