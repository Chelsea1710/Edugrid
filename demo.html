Interactive classroom prototype · Ireland

Make renewable electricity easier to understand.

Electricity charts and national energy figures can feel abstract. EduGrid turns the topic into a small experiment: change wind, solar and electricity demand, then see how the balance changes. It is designed to help secondary-school students explore the ideas behind the numbers—not to predict the real electricity grid.

The problem EduGrid explores

Big figures are hard to connect to everyday choices.

Students may hear that wind and solar are important, but it can be difficult to picture what happens when renewable output changes or people need more electricity. A simple, hands-on model gives the class something concrete to question, compare and discuss.

What this prototype does

It lets you test a simplified relationship between renewable supply and demand, compare illustrative records, and collect observations for a classroom discussion.

Important: the values and sample records are fictional teaching examples. This demo is not connected to EirGrid or any live electricity data.

01 · Start with a question

Questions the demo helps a class investigate

Try to change only one slider at a time. That makes it easier to notice which change affected the result.

1

What if the wind drops?

Lower wind while leaving solar and demand unchanged. Does the illustrative renewable coverage rise or fall? Why?

2

Can solar help on a sunny day?

Increase solar while keeping wind and demand fixed. Compare the result with your starting point.

3

Why does demand matter?

Raise demand without changing wind or solar. Can the same renewable supply cover a smaller share of a larger need?

What does the model show?

For this prototype, wind and solar are averaged into a single illustrative renewable-supply score. That score is compared with demand.

Illustrative renewable coverage
75%
Partial coverage

In this scenario, the simplified renewable-supply score covers about 75% of the demand score.

This calculation leaves out many real-world factors, including other generation sources, storage, imports, grid constraints, weather forecasts and timing. It is a learning aid only.

Classroom mini-investigation

  1. Write down the starting wind, solar and demand values, plus the coverage result.
  2. Choose one question above and change just one slider.
  3. Record the new result and describe what changed.
  4. Reset the demo, test a different condition, then compare your observations with a partner.

Discuss or write a short observation

Which example has the highest demand? Which has the strongest combined wind-and-solar score? Pick two rows and explain how their conditions differ. What important information would you need before drawing conclusions about the real electricity system?

04 · Learning goal

From abstract figures to testable questions

EduGrid is a proposed educational concept. Its goal is to help students practise reading data, testing one variable at a time, explaining patterns and asking what a simplified model leaves out. A future version could use verified public datasets and clearer links to curriculum topics.
